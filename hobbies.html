Outside work

Curiosity does not stop when the workday ends.

My interests outside formal work still revolve around learning, building, strategy, and staying disciplined—but without turning every activity into another résumé bullet.

05 / Interests and personal balance

Strategy + systems

Gaming

I enjoy games with progression, optimization, exploration, and systems that reward understanding how mechanics interact. It is a different context for the same kind of structured problem solving I enjoy in software.

Consistency

Fitness

Training provides a direct practice in discipline: progress is gradual, consistency matters more than intensity on one day, and the plan must adapt when recovery or circumstances change.

Independent study

Emerging technology

I spend time studying system design, local AI, distributed infrastructure, and quantum computing—not only what the technology can do, but the mathematics, architecture, and limitations underneath it.

Build to understand

Personal projects

Small applications are how I test ideas that are too abstract in notes alone. Building exposes missing requirements, hidden coupling, security assumptions, and the difference between a demo and a dependable system.

Science + perspective

Earth and space

Working with satellite data has made me more interested in how instruments observe Earth, how measurements become climate products, and how software helps scientists reason about systems larger than any one dataset.

Creative structure

Design

I enjoy turning dense information into interfaces that feel deliberate and easy to scan. Good design is not decoration; it is the organization of attention, decisions, and context.

Balance

Different activities develop different kinds of patience.

Software can create the illusion that every problem should resolve immediately. Fitness, games, long-form study, and creative projects reinforce the opposite lesson: improvement often comes from repeating fundamentals, recognizing patterns, and letting understanding accumulate.

That balance makes me more effective when I return to technical work. I can step away from a stubborn implementation, revisit the system model, and separate the actual problem from the first solution I became attached to.

LEARN

Read beyond the surface

Trace unfamiliar concepts back to their assumptions, definitions, and implementation details.

PRACTICE

Repeat the fundamentals

Use consistent training and focused exercises instead of relying on occasional bursts of motivation.

BUILD

Create something concrete

Turn an idea into a working project, then observe where the initial design fails.

RESET

Make room for perspective

Step away, recover, and return with enough distance to challenge the previous approach.

The common thread is simple: understand how things work, then make them better.

That applies to code, systems, training, games, and the way complex information is presented.
