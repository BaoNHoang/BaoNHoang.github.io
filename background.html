About

Adaptability shaped my background. Curiosity shapes my engineering.

I was born in Da Lat, Vietnam and moved to the United States during elementary school. Learning to adapt across languages, schools, and environments became part of how I approach difficult technical problems today.

My journey

Two cultures, one habit of continuous learning.

I grew up bilingual in English and Vietnamese after moving from Vietnam to the United States. That transition required patience, observation, and a willingness to keep learning even when the context was unfamiliar.

Those same habits now show up in my work. Scientific software requires learning the science behind the data. Full-stack engineering requires understanding how product, security, databases, APIs, and users affect one another. System design requires being comfortable with incomplete information and making trade-offs explicit.

I am most engaged when a project has a meaningful domain behind it—Earth science, research workflows, machine learning, or a product that helps people organize complex information.

“Learning never stops in the classroom. You have to go beyond it, absorb what the work demands, and build your own path forward.”

Engineering principles

How I want my work to be judged.

The best implementation is not the one with the most technology. It is the one that makes the system safer, clearer, and easier to evolve.

01

Understand the domain

Before changing code, learn what the data means, who uses it, and which assumptions cannot be violated.

02

Make boundaries explicit

Separate data processing, business rules, persistence, infrastructure, and presentation so each layer has a clear responsibility.

03

Design for failure

Use validation, timeouts, retries, privacy controls, useful errors, and observability instead of relying on ideal conditions.

04

Reduce repeated work

Turn one-off scripts and manual steps into reusable tools, documented workflows, and components other people can trust.

05

Communicate clearly

Architecture only creates value when scientists, engineers, stakeholders, and users can understand the decisions behind it.

06

Keep improving

Review assumptions, measure outcomes, accept corrections, and treat every project as a chance to improve the next one.
